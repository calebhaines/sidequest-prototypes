# A peculiar tale of time and tenderness

## The kingdom

The Land of Limited Time is not short of hours. It is short of permission to use them. Its settlement grew from parked trailers and temporary offices that were never replaced. Wire is spliced around dead meters, window plastic is taped over older plastic, and the rain travels through layers of repairs. Lady Supine's face appears on coupons, portraits, permits, and Channel 9. Every kiss on a coupon dissolves into a little more life. Every official repeats her promise: **Be Yourself. Be Divine.**

The inhabitants are part beast. Their supposed animal natures are used to excuse how little freedom they receive. A moth can be a poet, an ox a thoughtful porter, a hare a clockmender; the kingdom's forms contain no boxes for these things. The traveller and Supine appear entirely human. That distinction grants the traveller access while revealing the cruelty of the distinction itself.

Time is a currency, a ration, and a verdict. Timelessness is a crime because a person without a deadline is difficult to govern. The hospital is where those who run out are kept. The Library of Time Well Spent is where the evidence that they lived is kept.

## Tone

The prose takes official absurdities seriously. A gate really is unenterable. An island really can be a hole. A ruler really can change her mind by replacing it. Bureaucratic language conceals something frightening, while personal language makes room for tenderness. Humour arises from precise, unreasonable rules rather than from characters mocking the world.

Supine is a real living human ruler. Her head is disproportionately large, with a visible opening in the top through which a physical brain can be removed and replaced. The telescreens show her actual body; they do not constitute her existence. Her beauty is arresting and dangerous; the immense open head remains physically human. Her vanity is powerful and lonely. Her aesthetic doctrine denies the humanity of her subjects while asking every moment of their attention. She offers generosity most freely when it brings an audience. Asking for time reveals what her compliments have bought and what they never could buy.

The writing is materially bleak. Hunger, wet bedding, damaged hands, absent neighbours, and institutional neglect have consequences. The three friends remain kind, but kindness costs food and minutes they cannot comfortably spare. An ending can return an account balance; it cannot return the people who died while the account was withheld.

## Six acts

1. **The threshold.** Learn how time is assigned, work around the entrance, and discover three friends.
2. **The image.** Follow the willow song, gather scarce resources, and enter Supine's curated world.
3. **The wish.** Appreciate her art, encounter her through the telescreen, and ask for the one gift she refuses.
4. **The remedy.** Seek the hospital, the God Switch, and a humane replacement mind in the Pit of Reversion.
5. **The change.** Outwit the Aristobats, enter the sleeping chamber, and confront the consequences of the literal exchange.
6. **The entrance.** Accept the friends' offer without treating their lives as expendable, then decide what time should mean.

The journal exposes the actual campaign chapter names and objectives. These summaries describe the dramatic structure without fixing future expansion or final art direction.

## Friendship and consent

Pip, Brindle, and Ada have work, tastes, fears, and small pleasures independent of the traveller. Their friendship is not an inventory reward. Ada's participation in the mind exchange must be voluntary; a patient's replacement brain must not be harvested from an unwilling person. The horror lies in the system's indifference to bodies, not in the game approving that indifference.

The friends' final offer is emotionally central. It also presents a puzzle whose solution preserves a reserve for each friend. The traveller may seek infinite time, change the finite kingdom, or remain with the people they have met. No ending makes the friends' existence disposable.

The exchange is literal and visible. Ada’s brain travels into the hole in Supine’s head while Supine’s brain enters Ada’s hare body. That body adopts Supine’s cruelty and must be taken to the hospital. The return procedure visibly removes the ruler’s brain into a jar and restores Ada’s own mind. The original body is recognizable throughout; no abstract personality icon substitutes for the organs.

## Pacing

The standard story is paced for approximately five hours across six acts. It includes six mandatory, six-stage investigations alongside sixteen shorter story challenges, including fish caught in the actual lake. The investigations contain new accounts, witnesses, clues, discoveries, and consequences. The walkable world adds exploration, gathering, crafting, fishing, and Aristobat encounters; these are playable systems and can extend a leisurely playthrough.

The route-based audit counts 27,370 required reader words after the expanded narrative rewrite. At 220 words per minute, reading takes about 124 minutes; standard investigation thinking takes about 184 minutes; shorter story puzzles take about 29 minutes; navigation and decisions add about 15 minutes. The central estimate is approximately 352 minutes, or 5.9 hours. Standard assumptions give a range of approximately 5.1–6.7 hours. Optional branches add time; hints, assistance, skipped prose, and puzzle experience reduce it. These are reasoned estimates, not a timed human playtest. The reproducible inputs and results are in `scripts/pacing-report.json`.

Player time and fictional time are separate. Reading, consulting the journal, and considering a clue are free; active travel and Aristobats can spend borrowed time. All menus pause the world. The map sets a compass destination, and the traveller walks there. Exhaustion leads to care and debt, not an erased playthrough. This keeps scarcity present without charging for reading speed.

## The six casebooks

**The case of the missing tomorrow.** Nettle's mother Elin has been removed from the Library's public index. Establish where her afternoon went, reconstruct the order of events, and recover a claim the kingdom has declared nonexistent. Pip carries a kettle through the appeal.

**The abundance audit.** The Fields grow enough food; the Museum's orders manufacture shortage. Follow the shipments, interpret the dispatch, reconcile the shares, and release one harvest without claiming the whole country has already been repaired.

**The people beneath the portrait.** Recover five ordinary makers and subjects from paintings gradually promoted into Supine's divine authorship. Her beauty and capacity to learn remain real; the other people return to the work as people, making an honest compliment possible.

**The patient without a yesterday.** Morrow's missing date has been mistaken for a missing mind. Repair the ward's records, protect an interval of care, and rehearse a procedure in which every mind retains a name, voice, and way home.

**The listening castle.** The Aristobats can be relieved rather than murdered. Use the castle's neglected procedures to let workers eat, retire a public broadcast, preserve a return route, and give an endlessly watched woman a private room.

**A common tomorrow.** Trace the final escrow without counting people as expendable currency. Preserve protected reserves, distinguish conditional offers from ownership, and leave the final philosophical choice open.

Every case uses six distinct forms of reasoning: a cross-referenced ledger, an encoded account, a balance constrained by equations, a unique chronology, a route with ordered checkpoints and a finite distance, and a linked lamp circuit. The cases require all stages before the story can proceed. Evidence is self-contained, progressive hints are free, and an assisted board remains available for accessibility. Each input saves immediately, and completed work can be reviewed.

## Art direction, temporarily

An abstract top-down world drawn with fractured marker planes, asymmetric mask faces, broken contours, broad scratched gestures, and stained paper. Exactly 24 authored pigments are shared by scenery, figures, portraits, icons, maps, menus, and physical organs. Smooth antialiasing and transparent ink overlap blend the fixed pigments naturally. Dirt, cold grey-green water, brittle vegetation, faded cloth, and bruised brown dominate the palette. The old trailer park has been repeatedly extended using salvaged sheet metal, tarpaulins, signs, blocks, and stolen cable. Civic buildings retain those origins; Supine’s castle conceals them beneath an oversized facade. Water ripples, smoke moves, and tired people still walk. Tree roots remain planted while trunks bend, branch joints swing, separate canopy fragments drift, and willow strands trail behind the gust. Supine contrasts the broken settlement with elegant adult beauty; her enormous open skull and unwavering gaze carry the terror. No pixel art is used.

The original marker-style drawings and matching dialogue portraits establish composition and palette without locking in final artwork. Replacement illustrations can be registered without changing the game systems; scenery and portraits can be replaced independently after visual inspiration arrives.

Philip Guston, Cy Twombly, and Paul Klee guide the visual vocabulary: corporeal weight and soot outlines, nervous scrawls and erasure, brittle geometry and muted colour. These references describe the artwork rather than artists who exist in the game world.
