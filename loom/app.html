Make your first arrangement

Press Play to hear the prepared session. Select a track, choose a Music Lab instrument, and use its full editor. Arm the track with its round button, then Record to capture its output as an editable audio clip. Pause finishes the take and holds its position; Stop finishes it and returns to the beginning. The recording button can also finish a take and hold the position. Panic discards it.

Import audio directly into the selected track. Microphone recording asks for permission and records into the selected track without monitoring your microphone through the speakers. Recording continues across transport loops. The session extends to keep the complete take, up to 64 bars; a take stops after two minutes or at the 64-bar capacity, whichever comes first. Longer imported sources remain available through the clip’s source controls.

Give yourself a little warning

Count-in plays one or two bars of clicks before a new take starts, without recording the clicks. Punch in / out records only between the chosen boundaries while the arrangement plays around the take. Use the loop range as a quick starting point. Punch recording makes one pass through its range; an ordinary take can continue through transport loops.

Tell the needle where to go

Play starts or continues from the current position. Pause holds the position; Stop returns to the beginning. The lower transport row moves to the start or end, steps backward or forward by one bar, and restarts playback from the beginning. Click the clock to enter an exact bar, beat, and tick, click or drag the ruler to seek, or drag the needle’s handle. Seeking also moves playing audio and waits while recording is preparing, active, or finalizing.

Jump directly to Loop start or Loop end from the transport. Set the range with the numeric loop boundaries, or choose Loop this clip in the clip inspector. Follow keeps the moving needle in view; switch it off to explore another part of the arrangement. With Loop off, playback stops at the end of the session.

Move the pieces

Drag a clip to move it in time or to another track. Drag its edges to trim. The clip inspector offers precise timing, source boundaries, speed/pitch, reverse, loop, level, and fades. Split at the playhead or duplicate to build a phrase. Use Snap and Zoom to set the detail you need.

Sections and clip actions

Add named, colored Markers at the playhead, then click a section to jump there. Use its pencil button to rename, move, or delete it. Select a recording and use Delete clip directly below the timeline; Undo restores it. Right-click a clip for its actions. Send to instrument transfers its edited audio into a chosen FORM layer, MIRE source, SPOOL deck, RAVEL sample, or HAZE spectral score. Choose an empty track to load the recipient, or an existing matching instrument. Replacing an existing sample requires the replacement checkbox.

A shared sample cupboard

Samples opens a local library shared with the other Music Lab apps. Render an instrument or clip into the library, choose a sample to receive, and trim it to its destination’s limit. Portable sample packets preserve audio, tempo, and its source; WAV files work with other music software. The library uses this browser’s storage; save or download audio you want to keep. Samples received in LOOM become arranged audio clips.

Draw some movement

Select a track and an automation target, then add a lane. Click the curve to add a point and drag points to change their time or value; precise point controls work with a keyboard too. Choose Linear ramps or Hold steps. Switch a lane off to hear the manual setting. Arm Write while playback runs and move mixer or effect controls to record their movement. Write disarms when you switch tracks. Automation is saved in projects and included in WAV exports.

Mix and shape

Every track has level, pan, mute, solo, and exactly four effect slots. Select a slot to edit it; add any of the eight effects, bypass, reset, remove, or change its order. Effect order changes the sound. Live mode starts a loaded instrument alongside the DAW transport; turn it off when playing its recorded clips to avoid doubling.

Keep your work

Save/Open project includes audio, instrument settings, inserted effects, and uploaded instrument HTML. Undo/Redo restores arrangement, mixer, and effect edits. Browser recovery is optional when local storage is available. Stereo WAV export renders your arranged clips; you can export the master mix or individual stems, with an optional effect tail. Save a project before closing the browser.

Keys and accessibility

Space Play/Pause · Enter Stop and return to the beginning · Shift Space Play from the beginning · Home/End session start/end · Alt ←/Alt → move one bar · M Add marker · G Go to position · [/] jump to loop start/end · F Follow · L Loop · Shift L Loop selected clip · Escape Panic when no dialog is open · Ctrl/Cmd Z Undo · Ctrl/Cmd Shift Z Redo · Delete selected clip · D Duplicate · S Split. On a focused clip, arrow keys move by the snap interval, and Shift + arrow changes its length. Numeric controls are available for every clip edit.

Future instruments

Upload a standalone HTML Web Audio instrument, or load a same-origin URL. Existing Music Lab apps use an audio bridge to route their output through the selected track. Future apps can implement the documented Music Lab host interface for transport and project state. Untrusted instrument HTML should only be loaded when you trust its author.

LOOM uses eight audio tracks and four insert slots per track. Audio clips are the reliable arrangement format; live app performance is printed by recording. Instrument playheads may need a restart to line up a fresh take. Large projects consume browser memory. No audio is uploaded.
