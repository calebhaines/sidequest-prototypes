BRINE / OPERATING NOTES

Some ingredients like a little time.

Start with a sound

Choose a recipe, then play the keyboard or Play phrase. BRINE has six voices with a clocked DCO, sub oscillator, resonant four-pole filter and one shared envelope per voice. The analog character comes from the waveform, filter, amplifier and chorus paths, with stable DCO pitch.

Slide the ingredients

Move a slider or type its exact value below. Arrow keys adjust a slider; hold Shift for fine adjustment. Page Up/Down make larger moves, Home/End select the limits, and Enter or double-click restores the default. Each completed gesture is one Undo.

One oscillator, plenty of flavor

Saw, Pulse and Sub switches combine. Sub adds a square wave one octave below. Choose Manual, LFO or Envelope for pulse width, then set its amount. DCO LFO adds pitch movement; Delay lets the shared LFO arrive gradually. The 106 family has different pulse-width and high-pass behavior. AUTO uses the LFO delay after a new key phrase; MANUAL waits for the LFO trigger or modulation wheel.

Filter and contour

The global four-position HPF trims the mixed voices before the chorus. The 60 starts flat; the 106’s lowest position adds bass boost. Filter Envelope amount follows the selected positive or negative polarity. Keyboard tracking helps the filter follow pitch; VCF LFO adds movement. The shared ADSR controls both the filter and the amplifier in ENV mode. GATE uses a quick keyboard shape for the amplifier.

The chorus is part of the recipe

Try I for a slow wash, II for livelier motion, or the 60 family’s I + II for faster, shallower combined movement. The 106 family offers Off, I and II. Off leaves the voice dry. The modeled bucket-brigade delays, filters and stereo routing run in the same engine as exports. Inside the jar exposes the voicing and chorus details, including optional noise.

Play and pass it around

Connect MIDI for notes, velocity, pitch bend, modulation and sustain. The computer keyboard uses A W S E D F…; Z / X change octave. Space plays or stops the written phrase. Escape performs Panic.

The Keyboard arpeggiator turns played chords into an ordered part. Its Hold retains played notes; switch it off or use Panic to clear them. Arpeggio affects played keys and note/chord WAV exports. The written phrase and incoming GALLEY notes keep their own exact timing.

Write and compare

Open The phrase to draw polyphonic notes of any length. Drag notes to move, pull their right edges to resize, or select a note for exact settings. Tab selects notes; Enter/Space auditions one. Delete removes it; arrow keys move it, with Shift for an octave or beat.

A / B compares sounds while preserving the phrase, tempo, physical mod wheel and arpeggiator setup. Save/Open retains the whole project. Samples and Patterns connect it to the other Kitchen stations.

Take a little home

WAV export uses the actual synthesis and stereo chorus. Choose up to 64 bars; each render is limited to 120 seconds including its release tail, so split longer parts. Browser recovery is used when available. Published manuals and circuit references guide this original model; individual hardware-unit calibration remains unmeasured.
