# A peculiar tale of time and tenderness

## The kingdom

The Land of Limited Time is not short of hours. It is short of permission to use them. Lady Supine's face appears on coupons, portraits, permits, and Channel 9. Every kiss on a coupon dissolves into a little more life. Every official repeats her promise: **Be Yourself. Be Divine.**

The inhabitants are part beast. Their supposed animal natures are used to excuse how little freedom they receive. A moth can be a poet, an ox a thoughtful porter, a hare a clockmender; the kingdom's forms contain no boxes for these things. The traveller and Supine appear entirely human. That distinction grants the traveller access while revealing the cruelty of the distinction itself.

Time is a currency, a ration, and a verdict. Timelessness is a crime because a person without a deadline is difficult to govern. The hospital is where those who run out are kept. The Library of Time Well Spent is where the evidence that they lived is kept.

## Tone

The prose takes official absurdities seriously. A gate really is unenterable. An island really can be a hole. A ruler really can change her mind by replacing it. Bureaucratic language conceals something frightening, while personal language makes room for tenderness. Humour arises from precise, unreasonable rules rather than from characters mocking the world.

Supine's vanity is powerful and lonely. Her aesthetic doctrine denies the humanity of her subjects while asking every moment of their attention. She offers generosity most freely when it brings an audience. Asking for time reveals what her compliments have bought and what they never could buy.

## Six acts

1. **The threshold.** Learn how time is assigned, work around the entrance, and discover three friends.
2. **The image.** Follow the willow song, gather scarce resources, and enter Supine's curated world.
3. **The wish.** Appreciate her art, encounter her through the telescreen, and ask for the one gift she refuses.
4. **The remedy.** Seek the hospital, the God Switch, and a humane replacement mind in the Pit of Reversion.
5. **The change.** Outwit the Aristobats, enter the sleeping chamber, and confront the consequences of the literal exchange.
6. **The entrance.** Accept the friends' offer without treating their lives as expendable, then decide what time should mean.

The journal exposes the actual campaign chapter names and objectives. These summaries describe the dramatic structure without fixing future expansion or final art direction.

## Friendship and consent

Pip, Brindle, and Ada have work, tastes, fears, and small pleasures independent of the traveller. Their friendship is not an inventory reward. Ada's participation in the mind exchange must be voluntary; a patient's replacement brain must not be harvested from an unwilling person. The horror lies in the system's indifference to bodies, not in the game approving that indifference.

The friends' final offer is emotionally central. It also presents a puzzle whose solution preserves a reserve for each friend. The traveller may seek infinite time, change the finite kingdom, or remain with the people they have met. No ending makes the friends' existence disposable.

## The six casebooks

**The case of the missing tomorrow.** Nettle's mother Elin has been removed from the Library's public index. Establish where her afternoon went, reconstruct the order of events, and recover a claim the kingdom has declared nonexistent. Pip carries a kettle through the appeal.

**The abundance audit.** The Fields grow enough food; the Museum's orders manufacture shortage. Follow the shipments, interpret the dispatch, reconcile the shares, and release one harvest without claiming the whole country has already been repaired.

**The people beneath the portrait.** Recover five ordinary makers and subjects from paintings gradually promoted into Supine's divine authorship. Her beauty and capacity to learn remain real; the other people return to the work as people, making an honest compliment possible.

**The patient without a yesterday.** Morrow's missing date has been mistaken for a missing mind. Repair the ward's records, protect an interval of care, and rehearse a procedure in which every mind retains a name, voice, and way home.

**The listening castle.** The Aristobats can be relieved rather than murdered. Use the castle's neglected procedures to let workers eat, retire a public broadcast, preserve a return route, and give an endlessly watched woman a private room.

**A common tomorrow.** Trace the final escrow without counting people as expendable currency. Preserve protected reserves, distinguish conditional offers from ownership, and leave the final philosophical choice open.

## Art direction, temporarily

A top-down pixel world in moss, stone, old gold, and dark green, with parchment reading interfaces contained in the gameplay window. Water ripples, willow branches move, lanterns glow, creatures blink and walk, and wings open against the evening. Buildings are proud, crooked, or both. The world arrives through depth and movement while preserving the note’s bureaucratic unease and tenderness.

The procedural pixel art and matching dialogue portraits establish composition and palette without locking in permanent character designs. Replacement sprite sheets can be registered without changing the game systems; scenery and portraits can be replaced independently after visual inspiration arrives.
